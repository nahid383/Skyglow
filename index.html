Challenge Overview

Stellar Stories Challenge

Although the Sun is 93 million miles away from our planet, solar activity can significantly impact our daily lives. "Space weather"—the variations that occur in the space environment between the Sun and Earth—can impact technologies in space and on Earth.

Our Mission: Write and illustrate a digital children's story that explains what space weather is and the varying impacts it has on different people such as farmers, pilots, astronauts, power grid operators, and the general public.

We're telling the story from the perspective of a solar flare as it approaches Earth, showing children how this cosmic journey affects people in different walks of life.

People Affected by Space Weather:

👨‍🌾 Farmers
✈️ Pilots
👨‍🚀 Astronauts
⚡ Power Grid Operators
📡 Satellite Operators
🏠 General Public

Our skyglow research videos provide scientific backing for understanding how atmospheric phenomena and light pollution relate to space weather effects, helping us create an accurate and educational story.

What does SkyGlow do and how does it work?

SkyGlow is an interactive learning app concept designed to teach children about space weather — phenomena like solar flares, coronal mass ejections (CMEs), auroras, and geomagnetic storms — and how they affect our daily lives on Earth.

The app works through three learning levels:

🌟 Beginner Level

Introduces kids to basic concepts like auroras, solar flares, and satellites through playful animations and visuals.

📖 Intermediate Level

Story-driven adventures where children follow relatable characters like farmers, pilots, and power grid operators, showing how space weather affects GPS, radio communication, and electricity.

🔬 Advanced Level

For older learners, provides detailed simulations and research-based content, allowing users to explore NASA data and understand real impacts of solar activity.

Through interactive storytelling, children learn by seeing cause-and-effect in real-world scenarios. For example, when a solar storm hits, Mr. Alex's GPS-guided tractor zigzags, Captain Drake's airplane radio goes silent, and Windy the power grid operator watches warning lights flash.
